Abstract

Accurate segmentation of teeth from dental computed tomography (CT) images plays a crucial role in clinical diagnosis and treatment planning in dentistry. However, conventional segmentation methods often reach their limits, especially in complex cases involving missing teeth or existing prosthetic restorations. While deep learning methods are increasingly being utilized, comprehensive comparative studies of various segmentation models specifically for dental CT images remain scarce. The aim of this study is to address this research gap by systematically comparing several deep learning architectures, including UNet, SegNet, BASNet, BiSeNet, DenseASPP, and Mask R-CNN. The performance of these models was quantitatively evaluated using a self-created 2D dataset, employing the Dice Similarity Coefficient (DSC), precision, recall, and F1-score as primary evaluation metrics. The results facilitate a clear assessment of the different models, providing valuable guidance for clinicians and researchers in selecting the optimal deep learning model for clinical practice. Overall, this study establishes a foundational reference for future research in the field of dental imaging and segmentation.

Method

  • Data: MICCAI 2024 ToothFairy2 dataset (480 CBCT volumes, 42 classes). Because 3D training exceeded the available hardware, a new 2D dataset was built from selected axial slices.
  • Target: The 16 upper teeth (FDI 11–18, 21–28) plus background; irrelevant regions are excluded from the loss. Split: 80% training, 10% validation, 10% test.
  • Models: UNet, SegNet, BASNet, BiSeNetV2, DenseASPP and Mask R-CNN, all trained under identical conditions.
  • Loss: Cross-entropy + Dice (α = β = 1) with class weights based on inverse class frequency.
  • Training: Adam (lr 1e-4), batch size 8, mixed precision (GradScaler), up to 50 epochs, early stopping with patience 10; augmentation with rotation, scaling and flipping.

On the test set, BASNet achieved the highest Dice and F1 scores, thanks to its boundary-aware design. UNet and SegNet followed closely. BiSeNetV2 and DenseASPP struggled with fine anatomical details. Despite extensive investigation, Mask R-CNN failed to learn this dataset and could not be evaluated. A fine-tuning strategy with gradual encoder unfreezing gave no advantage over full training from the start.
